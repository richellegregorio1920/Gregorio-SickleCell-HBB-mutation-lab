## Disease Background

Sickle cell disease (SCD) is an inherited blood disorder caused by a harmful mutation in the HBB gene, which provides the instructions for making the beta chain of hemoglobin. Hemoglobin is an important protein found inside red blood cells that carries oxygen throughout the body. Normal adult hemoglobin (HbA) is made up of two alpha-globin chains and two beta-globin chains, which work together to carry oxygen properly.

One of the most common genetic variants associated with SCD is HbS, which results from a specific mutation known as HBB p.Glu6Val. This mutation replaces the normal amino acid glutamic acid with valine in the beta-globin protein.

When oxygen levels become low, this change causes HbS molecules to stick together and form rigid structures. As a result, normally flexible and round red blood cells can become stiff and develop a crescent or sickle shape. Because these abnormal cells are fragile, they can break down more quickly than normal red blood cells, which can lead to hemolytic anemia.

The rigid cells can also become stuck in small blood vessels, reducing blood flow and oxygen delivery to nearby tissues. This can cause painful episodes and may eventually lead to long-term organ damage.

Sickle cell disease is usually inherited in an autosomal recessive pattern, meaning that a person generally needs to inherit an altered gene from both parents to develop the disease. The most common and severe form is HbS/S disease, which occurs when a person inherits two copies of the HBB p.Glu6Val variant. Other forms include HbS/β-thalassemia and HbS/C disease.

## Gene and Normal Protein Function


The HBB gene provides the instructions for making the beta chain of hemoglobin. Beta-globin is part of adult hemoglobin, which is responsible for carrying oxygen from the lungs to tissues throughout the body.

The normal HBB coding sequence examined in this activity is 444 bp long and produces a predicted protein of 147 amino acids. The protein begins with `MVHLTPEEKS` and ends with `VANALAHKYH`.
