## 2. Documented HBB Mutation

The documented mutation examined in this activity was the HBB variant **c.20A>T**, written as **NM_000518.5(HBB):c.20A>T (p.Glu7Val)**.

| Parameter | Result |
|---|---|
| Gene | HBB |

**Interpretation:**  
The documented mutation affects only one nucleotide. The original sequence **GAG** changes to **GTG**, which results in a change from glutamic acid (E) to valine (V) at position 7. Because this is a substitution rather than an insertion or deletion, the reading frame stays the same. The protein also remains 147 amino acids long, and no premature stop codon is produced.

## 3. Artificial Mutation

An artificial mutation was also created to see how a different type of DNA change would affect the HBB sequence and protein.

| Parameter | Result |
|---|---|
| Mutation type | One-nucleotide deletion |
| Position deleted | Nucleotide 25 |
| Original nucleotide | A |
| Number of bases deleted | 1 |
| Predicted effect | Frameshift |
| Downstream amino acids | Many affected |
| Premature stop codon | Yes, around position 19 |

**Interpretation:**  
Unlike the documented mutation, the artificial mutation involved deleting one nucleotide. Even though only one base was removed, the deletion shifted the reading frame. This changed many of the amino acids that followed the mutation and eventually resulted in a premature stop codon around position 19. This shows how a small deletion can have a much larger effect on the resulting protein sequence.

## 4. Comparison of WT, Documented, and Artificial Mutations

| Parameter | WT HBB | Documented Mutation (c.20A>T) | Artificial Mutation (1-bp deletion) |
|---|---|---|---|
| CDS length | 444 bp | 444 bp | 443 bp |

| Expected functional consequence | Produces normal β-globin | Changes β-globin behavior and produces HbS | Greatly disrupts β-globin because of the frameshift and premature stop |

**Interpretation:**  
The comparison shows a clear difference between the two types of mutations. The documented c.20A>T mutation changes only one nucleotide and one amino acid, while keeping the reading frame and protein length unchanged. In contrast, the artificial one-base deletion changes the reading frame and affects many amino acids farther downstream. The artificial mutation also produces a premature stop codon, indicating a much broader change in the protein sequence.

---

## Interpretation
OThe result show that different types of mutations can have different effects on a protein. The documented c.20A>T substitution changes one amino acid, glutamic acid (E) to valine (V), without changing the reading frame or protein length. In contrast, the artificial one-nucleotide deletion causes a frameshift, affecting many downstream amino acids and producing a premature stop codon. These results demonstrate how a change in DNA can directly affect the resulting protein and its function.
